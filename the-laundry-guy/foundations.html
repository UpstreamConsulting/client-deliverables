02 — Brand

Positioning

One-liner · feels-like · voice

The Laundry Guy is a B2B cost-reduction, contract-enforcement, and invoice-intelligence brand. It is not a laundry service — it is a financial control layer for vendor spend. Closer to Ramp, Gong, and CXL than to anything that washes linens.

The one-linerpositioning

We help multi-location businesses reduce spend, recover credits, and stop invoice creep across vendors like Cintas, UniFirst, Vestis, Alsco, and ImageFirst.

Audience — CFOs, COOs, owners, Fixed Ops Directors, and procurement leaders across auto dealerships, hospitality, manufacturing, food processing, transportation, and healthcare.

Strategic positioningfeels like / does not feel like

Voice in one line

The Laundry Guy talks like the person in the room who has actually read the contract. Direct. Informed. Slightly provocative. Never fluffy.

03 — Logo

Lockups

4 lockups · mark = margin-leakage diagram

Four lockups: horizontal wordmark and standalone mark, each in color and reversed. The mark is intentionally a margin-leakage diagram disguised as a logo — an orange ring with navy dots escaping it. Use the mark at sizes ≥32px so the dots stay legible.

Sanctioned gradients — the only twoamendment 2026-08-02

Orange gradient--tlg-grad-orange · #E4713C → #C25925
Navy gradient--tlg-grad-navy · #01364F → #00202E

Same-hue, ~8% depth — a gradient that reads as two different colors side by side is too strong. Allowed on large surfaces (cards, bands, hero) and pill CTA buttons only. 07 — Radii

Corners

pill buttons · 0px invoices

Buttons and tags are full pills. Cards round softly at 10–14px, big surfaces at 20px. Invoice mockups stay at 0px on purpose — real invoices have hard edges. Don't round them.

12 — Voice

Voice & copy

lexicon · headlines · casing · numbers

Direct, informed, slightly provocative, never fluffy. Every piece teaches the reader something they didn't know about their own invoice. Specifics — dollars, line items, % change — do the persuading.

Rulescasing · numbers · no emoji

Headline patterns that work

  • Provocative question → blunt answer
  • Restated assumption → reframed reality
  • Number + verb ("30–50% recovered")
  • "What changed?" + receipts

Casing & numbers

  • Headlines: sentence case. Eyebrows: ALL CAPS +0.12em.
  • Dollars $3,481.20, tabular. Ranges 30–50% with en-dash.
  • No emoji. Ever. No exclamation marks — the data is the exclamation.
13 — Do-not

Off-brand patterns

avoid in every artifact

The patterns that break the brand on sight. Avoid these in every artifact — deck, ad, landing page, report, or dashboard.

Laundry imagery
No washing machines, soap bubbles, baskets, folded towels, or smiling delivery drivers.
Purple gradients
No multi-hue/SaaS gradients (two same-hue brand gradients are sanctioned — see Color). No glassmorphism, no glowing radial blurs.
Emoji
Never. Not in headlines, body, social, or product. Eyebrows and icons do the job.
Left-bar cards
No rounded cards with a colored left-border accent. Banned pattern.
Centered hero text
Marketing heroes are left-aligned, grid-locked. Centered reads generic-SaaS.
Square-corner buttons
Buttons are full pills (999px). Don't use boxy corners on CTAs.
Instinct check before ship

Strip the words. Could a reader tell this came from The Laundry Guy — or any AI tool? If "any AI tool," it fails.
